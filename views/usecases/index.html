01 Entrances

Count every visitor, at every door.

The question

Footfall is where every mall report starts, yet it is usually a guess from till receipts or a clicker at one door.

What SURDS reads

  • Each warm shape that crosses a line you draw across the doorway, and which way it went
  • Totals by the hour, the day and the week, per entrance
  • Which door carries the crowd at which time

What you do with it

Staff the hours that need it, show tenants real footfall, and compare a campaign week with the one before.

02 Storefronts

See who stops, not just who passes.

The question

A window display either stops people or it doesn't. Sales figures can't tell you which, because most people who stop never walk in.

What SURDS reads

  • Passers-by along the frontage
  • Shapes that slow down and stay at the glass, and for how long
  • The stop rate: stoppers out of everyone who passed

What you do with it

Rate window changes in days instead of seasons, compare frontages side by side, and bring numbers to rent reviews.

03 Seating & terraces

Know which tables are free, and for how long.

The question

Terraces and food courts overflow at lunch and sit empty at four. Nobody knows the real turnover or whether there are enough seats.

What SURDS reads

  • Which table areas have people at them
  • How long groups stay before a table frees up
  • Peak hours, day by day

What you do with it

Size seating to real demand, send staff to clear a table the moment it empties, and show free seats on a screen at the entrance.

04 Queues

Open the second till before the line gets long.

The question

Queues lose sales quietly. People look at the line, sigh and walk on, and no report ever records them.

What SURDS reads

  • How many people stand in the queue zone
  • How fast the front of the line moves, so roughly how long the wait is
  • The hot espresso machine too, which stays under the people threshold and is never counted

What you do with it

Alert staff when the line passes a limit, measure service speed, and plan shifts around the hours queues actually form.

05 Walking routes

Find the routes shoppers actually take.

The question

A floor plan shows where people should walk. Heat shows where they really do, and where nobody goes at all.

What SURDS reads

  • Positions over time, from a row of sensors along a walkway
  • The main paths, the shortcuts and the corners nobody visits
  • Bottlenecks where flow slows down

What you do with it

Put kiosks and promotions on the paths people use, fix dead corners with signage or seating, and stage events where the traffic is.

06 Capacity & safety

Keep busy spaces safe without a clicker at the gate.

The question

Rooftops, play areas and event spaces all have a safe limit. Counting by hand is slow, and it fails exactly when the space is busiest.

What SURDS reads

  • A live headcount across every sensor covering the space
  • How quickly it is filling up
  • How warm the crowd is getting

What you do with it

Warn the team at 80 percent, pause entry at the limit, and keep a record of every busy hour for safety audits.

07 Air-conditioning

Cool the people, not the empty wings.

The question

Air-conditioning runs to a timetable while the crowd moves around the building. An empty wing gets the same cold air as a packed one.

What SURDS reads

  • People in each zone, and how warm they are
  • The room temperature where they are
  • The AC's own remote codes, which it learns once and then sends itself

What you do with it

In Bloom, switch a zone to Automatic and pick its rules: on when someone is in, when people read warm, or when the room passes a limit. Off after a delay when no rule holds. Every switch is logged with its reason.

08 After hours & equipment

Watch the building when it's closed.

The question

After closing, cameras need someone watching hours of footage. A warm body in a closed area should simply raise its hand.

What SURDS reads

  • Any person in a zone during closed hours, in full darkness
  • The hottest point in view, always: motors, kitchen equipment, electrical panels

What you do with it

Send security to one alert instead of a wall of screens, and catch a motor running hot weeks before it fails.

09 Leasing & planning

Price every square metre on evidence.

The question

Rents are set on location and instinct. Busy and quiet units often pay the same, and prospects have to take your word for the traffic.

What SURDS reads

  • Footfall by wing and by frontage, week after week
  • Where people stop and where they only pass
  • The change a new anchor tenant or a refit really brings

What you do with it

Set rents and tenant mix on traffic, show prospects real numbers, and plan the next refit where it pays back.

Privacy by physics

It counts people. It can't see them.

SURDS has no camera. Its whole picture is 768 temperatures, a grid of 32 by 24. A person is a warm patch of a few cells. There is no face, no clothing and no screen in that data, so there is nothing to identify, leak or misuse.

  • Readings are turned into counts and positions on the sensor itself
  • Works in full darkness, because heat needs no light
  • Nothing to blur, mask or delete later

How it works

From box to live numbers in an afternoon.

  1. 1Mount it

    Screw the wall plate up high, looking across the floor people use, and click the sensor on. Its angled face tips it down into the space.

  2. 2Connect it

    Open Bloom in Chrome, connect to the sensor over Bluetooth and give it your Wi-Fi. The password goes straight to the sensor.

  3. 3Set the rules

    Tell Bloom how it hangs, set the people threshold, then switch on counting zones and air-conditioning rules.

  4. 4Read the numbers

    The live view shows people, temperatures and every decision. Analytics keeps the history, from 15 minutes to 7 days.

Questions

What people ask first.

Does it record video or images?

No. It has no camera. It reads a grid of 32 × 24 temperatures about twice a second and turns warm patches into counts and positions.

How does it tell people from hot things?

Each patch must be warmer than its surroundings by the blob threshold you set. A sunlit floor or an espresso machine sits under or outside that rule and is not counted, and the hottest point in view is reported separately so equipment is watched, not counted. Keep strong heat sources out of view where you can.

How many sensors do I need?

One for each area you want to measure: an entrance, a frontage, a seating area, a queue, a wing. Start with one door and add more where the questions are.

Does it work at night or in the dark?

Yes. It reads heat, not light, so it counts the same at midnight as at noon. That is what makes the after-hours alert possible.

How does it connect?

Over Wi-Fi, including company networks with a username and password. You set it up from Bloom in Chrome over Bluetooth, and the Wi-Fi password goes directly to the sensor.

Which air-conditioners can it run?

Units with an infrared remote. You teach it each button once in Bloom's Remote panel; it then sends the same codes itself and hears when someone uses the real remote.

Where do I see the data?

In Bloom, at thermal.gpunet.in: a live view of people, room and AC, a log of every decision with its reason, and Analytics from 15 minutes to 7 days.

Start with one door.

Put a SURDS Thermal over your busiest entrance for a week. The footfall alone usually answers the next question: where the second sensor should go.
