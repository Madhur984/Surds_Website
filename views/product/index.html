Sensor systems

Thermal sensor with IR blaster

Sees heat across a room, senses motion, and sends IR commands.

Orange + dark glass
Scroll. The sensor walks you through it.

01 See

Reads heat, not faces.

The thermal lens measures the temperature of everything in view: people, machines, windows, the air from a vent.

  • Every surface reports a temperature, from a 22°C wall to a 60°C charger.
  • Works in the dark. Heat doesn't need light to be seen.
  • A grid of numbers, never a photograph.

02 Sense

Knows who's in the room.

Body heat stands out clearly against a room, so the sensor knows how many people are there and where they're moving.

  • Counts people by their body heat, about 33°C at the skin.
  • Tracks movement as warm shapes cross the room.
  • Notices a room emptying, and how long it has been empty.

03 Act

Talks to your AC for you.

The IR blaster sends the same signals as the remote in your drawer, so the ACs, fans and TVs you already own respond without rewiring.

  • Learns the codes from your existing remote.
  • Sends on, off, mode and setpoint, like pressing the buttons yourself.
  • Logs every command to the SURDS mesh, with the reason.

04 Trust

Anonymous by design.

Low-resolution heat, processed on the device. It can't capture a face, so there's nothing personal to leak.

  • No image sensor that could record a face, a screen or a document.
  • Thinks on the device. Counts and actions leave it; frames don't need to.
  • Readable rules. You can always see why it did what it did.

In one sentence

A small box on your wall that watches a room's heat, works out who's there and what's running hot, and tells your AC what to do, without a camera ever seeing a face.

The decision loop

Sense. Decide. Act.
Every second, on the device.

Nothing is streamed to a cloud to be interpreted. The sensor does the thinking where the heat is.

  1. 1

    Sense

    The thermal lens takes a fresh reading of the room: a grid of temperatures.

    Out: “2 warm bodies, desk 3 and 5. Room 27.4°C.”

  2. 2

    Decide

    Simple rules you can read run on the sensor itself. They keep working when the internet doesn't.

    Out: “Occupied and 3.4°C over target. Start cooling.”

  3. 3

    Act

    It fires the IR command at the AC, and reports what it did to the SURDS mesh so your team sees every action.

    Out: “IR → AC: COOL 24°C.” and a log line on your dashboard.

Privacy

It sees heat.
Not people.

Most “smart” sensors are cameras with software on top. This one physically can't take a picture.

  • No image sensor. The thermal array is low resolution by design. Faces and text don't exist in its output.
  • Processed on the device. Counts, temperatures and actions leave the sensor. Raw frames don't need to.
  • Readable rules. You can see exactly why it switched something on or off. No black box.

Questions

Asked before
every pilot.

Is it a camera?

No. It has a thermal array that reports a coarse grid of temperatures. There's no image sensor that could capture a face, a screen or a document.

What can it control?

Anything that takes an infrared remote: most split and window ACs, many fans, heaters and TVs. If your remote has to point at it, the sensor can too.

Does it need the internet?

No. Sensing and rules run on the device, so a room keeps behaving when the connection drops. The SURDS mesh is how it reports and how you change its rules.

Where should I mount it?

High on a wall or on the ceiling, facing the area people use, with a clear line of sight to the AC for the IR blaster.

How many do I need?

Usually one per room. Open floors need roughly one per 40 m². We map it with you during pilot scoping.

Can it tell who someone is?

No. It knows a warm body is there and roughly where. Identity isn't in the data, so it can't be inferred later either.
