Bloom Thermal · Complete tutorial

Learn Bloom in one sitting.

Bloom watches a room through a small thermal sensor, counts the people in it by their body heat, and switches the air conditioning for you through infrared, the same way your remote does. This tutorial takes you from an unboxed sensor to a room that runs itself.

1

It sees heat

A 32 × 24 grid of temperatures, about two frames a second. Never a picture, never a face.

2

It counts people

Warm patches become people. It knows how many are in, and how warm they read.

3

It runs the AC

Simple rules you set decide when the AC turns on and off, and every decision is logged with its reason.

How to use this tutorial. Work top to bottom, or jump to a chapter from the list. Every chapter ends with Mark as done; your progress is remembered in this browser. The grey Practice boxes are safe copies of Bloom's controls, so try them freely: nothing here touches your real sensor.

Chapter 3

Connect the node to Wi-Fi

The sensor gets its Wi-Fi details from your phone or laptop over Bluetooth. You only do this once per network. The password goes straight to the device; Bloom's server never sees it.

  1. Open Bloom in Chrome at https://thermal.gpunet.in and stand near the sensor.
  2. Press Device Wi-Fi in the top bar. The setup window opens.
  3. Press Connect to device. Chrome shows its Bluetooth list; pick the thermal node and press Pair.
  4. Press Scan for networks to let the sensor list the networks it can hear, or type the name into Network name (SSID).
  5. Choose the Security type your network uses (see the table below) and fill in what it asks for.
  6. Press Save and connect. The sensor saves the details and restarts onto the new network.
  7. Close the window and watch the status pill: within a minute it should show your device name and up … s.

Which security type?

Option in BloomUse it forFields to fill

WPA3 Personal onlyNewer routers set to WPA3 onlyNetwork name, password

Open (no password)Networks with no passwordNetwork name only
Enterprise networks: the extra fields explained
Username
Your network login, as your IT team gave it to you.
Outer identity
Optional. Some networks want an anonymous name on the outside of the login. Leave empty and Bloom uses your username.
EAP method
Auto tries PEAP and TTLS. Pick one only if IT tells you to.
Inner (TTLS)
Only for TTLS: MSCHAPv2 is the usual one; PAP, CHAP and MSCHAP are there if IT asks.
Switching networks later. Bluetooth setup is always on, so you can move the sensor to another network any time: open Device Wi-Fi, connect, and save new details. Forget Wi-Fi wipes the saved network from the sensor.
“Bluetooth setup needs the secure page”? You opened Bloom over plain http or in a browser without Web Bluetooth. Open https://thermal.gpunet.in in Chrome and try again.

Chapter 5

Mount the sensor and set its orientation

Where the sensor hangs decides what it can count. Then three buttons under Device orientation tell Bloom how it hangs, so the view on screen matches the room.

Where to mount it

  • High up, on the ceiling or high on a wall, looking down across the area people use.
  • Away from heat in view: radiators, heaters, coffee machines and sunny windows look like warm bodies.
  • Line of sight to the AC so its infrared commands reach the AC's receiver.
  • Within good Wi-Fi: check the signal in the bottom strip after mounting.

The orientation buttons

Set as facing down
Hold or mount the sensor the way it will hang, lens to the floor, then press it. Bloom remembers that pose and shows tilt relative to it.
zero yaw
Makes the sensor's current heading the zero point, so the yaw reading starts at 0°.
Rotate view
Turns the thermal view and the people a quarter turn each press (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°). Press until someone walking toward the door moves toward the door on screen.
Roll · pitch · yaw
The live tilt of the sensor. The small cube labelled TOP and LENS turns with it.

Chapter 6

Read the room panel

The left panel is the room in numbers. One setting lives here too: the blob threshold, which decides what counts as a person.

LineWhat it tells you
people and room °CThe headline: how many are in and how warm the room is.
People in the zoneHow warm the people read: the average of each person's hottest point. Shows nobody when the room is empty. Skin reads around 32–35 °C.
Hottest pointThe single warmest spot anywhere in view. A sudden high value can mean equipment running hot.
Sensor boardThe temperature of the sensor's own electronics. It runs warmer than the room; that is normal.
Blob thresholdHow much warmer than its surroundings a patch must be to count as a person, in °C. Default 2.

Rule of thumb. If chairs, laptops or a window get counted, raise the threshold by 0.5 °C at a time. If someone sitting still is missed, lower it. Small changes go a long way.

Chapter 7

The thermal view

Press Show thermal view in the top bar to lay the sensor's own picture over the watched room. Press it again (it then reads Hide thermal view) to go back to the people markers.

  • It is coarse on purpose. 32 × 24 temperature readings: enough to find people, too few to ever show a face.
  • Colours run cold to hot: dark purple, red, orange, yellow, then white for the warmest.
  • People markers hide while the thermal view is on, because the view already shows the people.
  • The room tag on the picture (Open office · 1 in · 27.0 °C) is the same headline as the Room panel.
  • On a phone Bloom opens on the whole building, then glides into the watched room. Use Zoom to the room on the picture to go out and in again, or tap the building.

Chapter 8

Automate the AC

The right panel decides when the AC runs. With Automatic on, Bloom turns the AC on when any rule you have switched on is true, and off once none is true for the delay you set.

RuleTrue whenGood for
On when someone is inAt least one person is countedMeeting rooms, cabins
On when people read ≥ X °CThe people's average hottest point is at or above XCooling only when people actually feel warm
On when room ≥ Y °CThe room is at or above Y, people or notServer rooms, keeping a space below a limit
Off after empty for N s(not a rule) how long Bloom waits, once no rule holds, before switching offAvoiding off/on flicker when someone steps out

    Taking over by hand

    Turn on and Turn off override the rules for a while; the status line adds manual for … s. When you want the rules back sooner, press Resume auto, which only appears during an override. Try it in the simulator above.

    A good starting setup: Automatic on, On when someone is in on, On when room ≥ 26 °C on, and Off after empty for 120 s. Then tune with a week of Analytics.

    Chapter 9

    Teach Bloom your AC's remote

    The sensor has an infrared receiver that listens to your remote, and a blaster that can press its buttons for you. Teach it each button once.

    1. Hold a button in Bloom's Remote panel for about half a second. Its label changes to press remote…
    2. Point your real remote at the sensor and press the same button.
    3. The card flashes and now reads mapped. Bloom has learned that code.
    4. Tap a mapped card to send the code to the AC, just like pressing the remote.

      Chapter 10

      Read the event log

      Under the remote is Bloom's diary, newest first. Every AC change says why, so you never have to guess.

      Line in the logWhat happened
      AC ON: 1 in the roomThe someone is in rule switched the AC on.
      AC ON: room 25.7 °C ≥ 24.0The room rule fired: 25.7 °C passed your 24 °C line.
      AC OFF: no rule holds (room 28.1 °C, people 34.6 °C) for 37 sNothing switched on was true for longer than your off delay, so Bloom switched off. The values are what it saw.
      AC ON: manual (page) / AC OFF: manual (page)Someone pressed Turn on or Turn off on the page.

      sensor connected: live valuesThe sensor came back; numbers are real again.
      sensor not connected: showing demo valuesThe sensor dropped out; Bloom switched to demo values.
      device offlineNo messages from the sensor. Check power and Wi-Fi.

      Chapter 11

      Analytics: look back in time

      Press Analytics in the top bar. Pick a range at the top (15 min, 1 h, 6 h, 24 h or 7 days) and read the room's history. Live view takes you back.

      • Four tiles at the top: Room now, People now, In the room and AC on, each for the chosen range.
      • Room and people temperature: the room (blue) and the people (orange). The dashed lines are your AC thresholds, so you can see when a rule would fire.
      • People in the room, and when the AC ran: the grey area is occupancy, the blue bars are when the AC was on. Gaps between people and blue bars are wasted cooling.
      • Smoothed, not hidden. Lines are moving averages so a week reads as a trend; the raw readings stay faint behind them. A gap in the data breaks the line.
      • Hover (or touch) a chart for exact values at that moment, on both charts at once. Open Table for the numbers.

      Chapter 12

      Troubleshooting

      Find your symptom. Each answer is in the order to try things.

      It says “Sensor not connected · demo”
      1. Check the sensor has power.
      2. Open Device Wi-Fi and reconnect it to your network (chapter 3).
      3. Watch the log for sensor connected: live values.
      Device Wi-Fi does nothing, or says it needs the secure page
      1. Use Chrome on Android, Windows, macOS or Linux. Safari, Firefox and iPhones cannot do Web Bluetooth.
      2. Open exactly https://thermal.gpunet.in (https, not http).
      3. Make sure Bluetooth is on and you are within a few metres of the sensor.
      Saved the Wi-Fi but the sensor never comes online
      1. Re-check the password and the security type; Enterprise networks need the username.
      2. Use Scan for networks: if your network is not listed, the sensor cannot hear it from where it is.
      3. Many sensors only join 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi; check your router offers it.
      It counts people who are not there
      1. Turn on the thermal view and look for warm things that are not people: laptops, heaters, sunny glass.
      2. Raise Blob threshold by 0.5 °C at a time (chapter 6).
      3. Re-aim the sensor so the heat source is out of view.
      It misses people who are sitting still
      1. Lower the blob threshold by 0.5 °C.
      2. Check the sensor looks down on the seats rather than across them.
      People move the wrong way on screen
      1. Press Rotate view until movement matches the room (chapter 5).
      2. Mount the sensor, then press Set as facing down and zero yaw.
      The AC does not react to Bloom
      1. Check the card in Remote says mapped; if not, teach it again (chapter 9).
      2. Make sure nothing blocks the line between sensor and AC.
      3. Look at the log: if Bloom says AC ON but the AC stays off, it is the infrared path, not the rules.
      The AC switches off and on too often
      1. Raise Off after empty for to 120 s or more.
      2. Use the people-temperature rule instead of the room rule if the room sits right at your threshold.
      Analytics says “No readings in this range yet”
      1. The sensor has not reported during that range. Pick a longer range, or check the sensor is live.

      Glossary

      Thermal node
      The sensor board: thermal camera, IR receiver and blaster, orientation sensor, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth.
      Thermal frame
      One 32 × 24 grid of temperatures. Bloom gets about two a second.
      Blob
      A patch warmer than its surroundings. Blobs over the threshold are counted as people.
      Demo mode
      Generated values Bloom shows when no sensor is connected, always labelled as demo.
      Setpoint
      The temperature line a rule compares against, such as 24 °C in On when room ≥ 24.
      Override
      A manual Turn on / Turn off that pauses the rules for a while.
      RSSI / dBm
      Wi-Fi signal strength. −50 is excellent, −70 is fine, −80 is weak.
